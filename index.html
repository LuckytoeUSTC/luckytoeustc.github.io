Research interests

My interest in theoretical physics begins with wanting to understand nature and its beauty. To me, theory is a way to uncover the regularities of nature and, through them, the structure of the world. Questions about symmetry, symmetry breaking, and emergence continue to fascinate me.

I would like to grasp, and perhaps develop, mathematical structures that help solve concrete problems, and to understand the mysterious connections between different theories. Working through real problems from different fields should also give me a more visual understanding of the mathematical objects involved. The research I admire most starts from a phenomenon, abstracts a clearly stated mathematical problem, solves it, and returns to the phenomenon with an explanation.

Theoretical physics taught me to look for structures shared by very different systems, and I would like to bring that approach to neuroscience. I want to understand how connectivity and dynamics produce collective activity in a network, and how that activity supports learning, memory, and attention. I am interested in how the covariance spectrum, as a general tool, can help explain cognitive processes across experiments. Beyond that, I am curious about the neural basis of language and how the words we use shape the way we think. I would also like to see how the theory changes when I change the assumptions about nonlinear dynamics or connectivity.
